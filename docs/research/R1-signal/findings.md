# R1 — Technical Signal / Strategy Edge: Findings

**Track:** R1 — Technical signal / strategy edge (what real, testable edge to build first, and which strategy to start with)
**Scope:** A fully-automated, deterministic technical bot for FTMO. No fundamentals (that is R8), no LLM on the hot path. Instrument #1 is **EURUSD** (decided in R2). The deliverable is a SIGNAL: its entry/stop/exit logic, its regime-awareness, its expectancy hypothesis, and a clear recommendation for the first strategy to build. Sizing and hard limits are owned by the Risk Governor (R4), not here.
**Research date:** 2026-06-02. Empirical claims are cited inline; folklore is flagged as such. All expectancy numbers below are **hypotheses to validate in backtest (R6), not promises.**

---

## 1. Summary

The single most important lesson from the prior failed system (NNFX — a fixed indicator stack with **no regime filter**) is that on intraday FX majors, *the edge is not in the indicator, it is in knowing when to stand aside.* EURUSD spends most of its intraday life in low-efficiency chop where every trend-following flip bleeds an ATR-multiple, and most of its realizable directional movement in a few hours per day. So the first build should not be "a better indicator" — it should be **a regime gate wrapped around a simple, well-understood signal**, traded only in the conditions and hours where that signal has historically had a chance to clear costs.

Concretely, my recommendation is to build, first, a **session-gated London/NY-overlap breakout (opening-range / volatility breakout) on 15m EURUSD, hard-gated by a cheap deterministic regime filter (Kaufman Efficiency Ratio + ATR-vs-baseline volatility, with ADX as a secondary confirm), that stands flat outside the overlap and stands flat when the gate says "chop."** Breakout is the approach whose *one* genuinely-documented edge on FX majors — the London/NY session volatility expansion — is a real, time-localized, exploitable phenomenon rather than folklore, and it pairs naturally with a regime gate. A trend-following SuperTrend variant (the family the reference script belongs to) is the reasonable **second** candidate but is whipsaw-prone intraday and should only enter the build after the regime gate is proven, because *the gate is what makes either approach survive.*

On the reference Pine script ("Self-Aware Trend System" / SATS): its **Trend Quality Index (TQI) is a genuinely useful regime measure in concept**, and the Efficiency-Ratio and momentum-persistence factors are legitimate. But the script as a whole is **not trustworthy evidence** of edge (it is an indicator with no cost model and no real backtest — its on-chart "performance stats" are meaningless), its SuperTrend-flip entry is laggy and whipsaw-prone on EURUSD intraday, several TQI factors are weak or partly circular (the "structure" factor especially), and the character-flip and self-learning machinery are unvalidated curve-fit surface area. **Verdict: mine the TQI idea (specifically ER + vol-regime) as the basis of our deterministic regime gate; do NOT adopt the SuperTrend-flip entry or the character-flip/auto-calibration logic as a candidate strategy.** Use it as a *regime-filter inspiration*, not as a strategy to port.

## 2. What edge to exploit: the five approaches, evenhanded

The honest starting premise (consistent with R2): **none of these instruments or approaches hands you a free directional edge.** FX majors are close to a random walk at most scales; what little structure exists is small, conditional, and easily eaten by costs. So the question is not "which approach is magic" but "which approach has a real, conditional, *time- or regime-localized* edge that survives EURUSD's ~1.1-pip all-in round-trip cost." Here is each, with where it works and where it fails.

**Trend-following (SuperTrend / MA-cross / Donchian-trail).** Works when a real directional impulse persists — which intraday on EURUSD means the London/NY overlap and the hours around scheduled US data. Fails badly in the 70–80% of time FX spends in consolidation, where each flip costs roughly the band's ATR-multiple; *"every flip in a range costs you the ATR-multiple distance, and in choppy weeks, Supertrend bleeds"* ([TrendSpider](https://trendspider.com/learning-center/supertrend-indicator-a-comprehensive-guide/), [Pineify](https://pineify.app/resources/blog/pine-script-supertrend-a-comprehensive-guide)). Characteristic profile: **low win rate (~30–45%) carried by a few large winners with 2.5:1–4:1 reward:risk** ([adventuresofgreg](https://adventuresofgreg.com/blog/2026/01/19/ultimate-guide-profitability-win-loss-ratios/)). It is robust *only* when paired with a regime/volatility filter that keeps it out of chop — *"using moving averages or ADX as filters helps avoid these whipsaws"* ([TrendSpider](https://trendspider.com/learning-center/supertrend-indicator-a-comprehensive-guide/)). Unfiltered intraday trend-following on a major is essentially the NNFX failure mode.

**Mean-reversion.** Mean reversion is genuinely *present* in intraday EURUSD — it is detectable even at the 1-minute scale ([ResearchGate: EURUSD Intraday Price Reversal](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/279276207_Eurusd_Intraday_Price_Reversal)) — and it benefits from FX spending most of its time consolidating, giving it high opportunity frequency and high win rates (60–80%) ([Axiory](https://www.axiory.com/trading-resources/strategies/mean-reversion-vs-trend-following), [QuantifiedStrategies](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/mean-reversion-strategies/)). But it has two fatal properties for *this* project. First, the win rate is a trap: average loss tends to exceed average win, and *"when trends do emerge they can wipe out months of mean-reversion profits in days"* — a fat left tail, which is exactly the kind of event a 5%-daily / 10%-max FTMO envelope cannot tolerate. Second, the patterns are weak and the academic consensus is that *"it would be difficult to profitably exploit these weak patterns in the face of trading costs"* ([arXiv: Trends and Reversion across time scales](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.16772v1)) — the reversion edge is largest at the shortest scales where costs are largest. Mean-reversion is a *later* candidate, not a first build, precisely because its tail risk is the worst fit for the drawdown rules.

**Breakout (opening-range / volatility breakout).** This is the approach with the cleanest, most-documented *conditional* edge on FX majors, because it is anchored to a real structural phenomenon: the **London open and the London/NY overlap concentrate liquidity and produce repeatable volatility expansion.** *"The London open is the single most consistent window for breakout trading in forex"* and *"the London/NY overlap frequently produces strong intraday trends, breakouts, or sharp reversals"* ([NewYorkCityServers: London Breakout](https://newyorkcityservers.com/blog/london-breakout-strategy), [Maven: sessions](https://maventrading.com/blog/forex-trading-sessions-hours-volatility-guide)). The well-known weakness is **false breakouts** — pure ORB win rates run ~40–60% and the main drawback is *"a high number of false breakouts when the strategy is used in its pure form"* ([LiteFinance](https://www.litefinance.org/blog/for-beginners/trading-strategies/opening-range-breakout-strategy/), [NordFX](https://nordfx.com/en/traders-guide/ORB-strategy)). The standard, evidenced mitigations are exactly the tools we already want: ATR/volatility confirmation, a session/time gate, and a regime filter ([FXOpen](https://fxopen.com/blog/en/opening-range-breakout-strategy/)). Breakout's edge is *time-localized and filterable*, which is what makes it the best first candidate.

**Session / time-of-day.** Strictly speaking this is not a standalone strategy but a powerful **conditioning variable** that should sit on top of whichever signal we pick. The evidence here is strong and consistent: the **London/NY overlap (~13:00–17:00 GMT, 08:00–12:00 ET) carries the highest median volatility and roughly half the day's volume**, and the NY open (08:30 ET data) produces repeatable bursts ([Maven: sessions](https://maventrading.com/blog/forex-trading-sessions-hours-volatility-guide), [OANDA](https://www.oanda.com/us-en/trade-tap-blog/trading-knowledge/when-is-the-best-time-for-forex-trading/), [TradeThatSwing: EURUSD volatility](https://tradethatswing.com/analyzing-eur-usd-volatility-for-day-trading-purposes/)). Mid-week (Tue–Thu) gives the most reliable fills. The "edge" is that *moves large enough to clear costs are not uniformly distributed across the clock* — they cluster in the overlap. Standing flat in the Asian session and at the 5pm-ET rollover (where even majors gap and spreads widen, per R2) is itself a source of saved cost and avoided slippage. This is folklore-adjacent only in its overstatement ("trade the open and print money"); the underlying volatility-clustering is real and statistical.

**Volatility-regime.** Also not a standalone strategy but the second key conditioning variable: classify each bar as low/normal/high realized-vol (ATR vs a longer ATR baseline) and as trending/choppy (Efficiency Ratio / ADX), and only let the signal fire in the regime it was built for. This is the *direct antidote to the NNFX failure*. The robust, evidenced claim is simply that **different strategies work in different regimes and fail in the wrong one** ([FractalCycles: Hurst vs ADX](https://fractalcycles.com/compare/hurst-vs-adx), [TradingView: Volatility Regime Classifier](https://www.tradingview.com/script/zagpmoKH-Volatility-Regime-Classifier-QuantRegime/)) — so the regime layer is not optional polish, it is the load-bearing part of the system.

**Robustness ranking for an intraday EURUSD first build (after costs):** session-gated **breakout** ≳ regime-filtered **trend-following** ≫ **mean-reversion** (tail-risk-disqualified for a first build under FTMO rules), with **session** and **volatility-regime** as mandatory conditioning layers on whichever signal wins. The folklore to discard: that any single indicator stack is profitable "as-is" without a regime gate (the NNFX premise), and that high win rate (mean-reversion) equals safety (it hides the left tail).

## 3. Regime detection: cheap, deterministic, non-repainting methods

The regime layer must be **computable on bar close from completed bars only** (no look-ahead, no repaint), cheap, and robust. Here are the candidate measures ranked by suitability for our hot path.

**Kaufman Efficiency Ratio (ER) — recommended primary.** ER = |close − close[n]| / Σ|close − close[1]| over n bars: net directional travel divided by total path length, bounded [0,1]. High ER = clean directional move; near-zero ER = choppy/inefficient. It is *one division*, fully deterministic, and uses only past closes, so it cannot repaint. It is purpose-built to *"help traders avoid choppy markets and identify smoother trends"* ([TradingPedia: Kaufman ER](https://www.tradingpedia.com/forex-trading-indicators/kaufmans-efficiency-ratio/), [QuantConnect: KER](https://www.quantconnect.com/docs/v2/writing-algorithms/indicators/supported-indicators/kaufman-efficiency-ratio)). This is exactly the right primary gate: a breakout/trend signal should fire only when ER over a short window (e.g. ~14–20 bars) is above a threshold (e.g. >0.30–0.50), and the bot should stand aside below it.

**ATR-vs-baseline volatility regime — recommended secondary.** ratio = ATR(short) / SMA(ATR, long); >~1.3 = expansion (volatility burst, breakout-favourable), <~0.7 = compression (chop/coil). Trivially cheap, deterministic, non-repainting. This is the second axis the strategy conditions on and is the *direct* measure of "is there enough range right now for a move to clear the spread." It pairs with the session gate: vol expansion *at the overlap* is the green light.

**ADX — recommended confirm, not primary.** ADX measures directional-movement strength: >25 moderate trend, >40 strong, <20–25 weak/rangebound ([FractalCycles: Hurst vs ADX](https://fractalcycles.com/compare/hurst-vs-adx)). It is deterministic and standard, but it **lags** (it is built on smoothed DI averages) and rises *after* a trend is underway, so it is better as a secondary confirmation than as the leading gate. Useful as a veto ("don't take a breakout if ADX says the prior regime was dead-flat and is not waking up") rather than the trigger.

**Choppiness Index — usable, overlaps ER.** Compresses ATR sum relative to the window's high-low range into 0–100 (high = choppy, low = trending). Deterministic and non-repainting, but it is conceptually close to ER and adds little once ER + ATR-ratio are in place; treat it as optional cross-check, not core ([ForexFactory: choppy market index](https://www.forexfactory.com/thread/512048-choppy-market-index-any-good-ideas)).

**Hurst exponent — informative offline, avoid on the hot path.** H>0.55 persistent/trending, ≈0.5 random walk, <0.45 mean-reverting ([FractalCycles: Hurst vs ADX](https://fractalcycles.com/compare/hurst-vs-adx)). It is the most *theoretically* principled regime measure, but the rescaled-range / DFA estimators are expensive, noisy on short intraday windows, and sensitive to estimator choice — too unstable bar-to-bar to gate live trades. Use Hurst in **R6 research** to characterize regimes and validate that ER/ATR thresholds line up with persistence; do not put it on the deterministic hot path.

**Autocorrelation of returns — research only.** Lag-1 autocorrelation distinguishes trending (positive) from mean-reverting (negative), but it is noisy on short windows and better as an offline diagnostic than a live gate.

**How the strategy "stands aside."** The gate is a hard precondition checked on each completed bar: if (session ∉ overlap window) OR (ER < ER_min) OR (ATR-ratio < vol_min) OR (an ADX/structure veto fires), **no order is placed and any pending breakout order is cancelled.** Standing aside is the default state, not the exception — the bot should be flat far more often than it is in a trade. This is the single design choice that most directly fixes the NNFX failure: regime-awareness is a *gate*, not a weighting tweak.

## 4. Candid evaluation of the reference script (SATS / "Self-Aware Trend System")

I read the source (Pine v6, ~1,200 lines). The core is an **ATR SuperTrend whose band multiplier is modulated by a Trend Quality Index (TQI ∈ [0,1])**, a weighted blend of four factors, plus character-flip exit logic, three R-multiple TPs, a display-only 0–102 "signal score", and an OFF-by-default self-learning calibrator. Verdict factor by factor.

**TQI as a regime measure: genuinely useful in concept, with caveats.** The idea — compress bands when trend quality is high (trail tighter in clean trends), widen them when quality drops — is sound and is essentially a continuous regime filter, which is the right instinct and the part worth mining. But it is bolted onto a SuperTrend *entry*, and the entry is the weak part (below). Critique of each factor:

- **Efficiency Ratio (weight 0.35) — legitimate.** This is Kaufman's noise/trend measure, the same one I recommend as our primary gate (§3). The highest weight is correctly placed. Genuinely predictive of "is this a clean move."
- **Volatility regime = ATR/SMA(ATR) (weight 0.20) — legitimate.** Same ATR-vs-baseline measure I recommend as secondary. Reasonable and cheap.
- **Structure = |pricePosition − 0.5| × 2 (weight 0.25) — weak and partly circular.** pricePosition is the close's location in the N-bar high-low range; this factor rewards price being *at the extremes* of its recent range. The problem: at a range extreme you are, by construction, either at a fresh breakout (good) **or** at an over-extended exhaustion point about to mean-revert (bad) — the factor cannot tell these apart, yet it scores both as "high quality." It is also mechanically correlated with where a SuperTrend flip happens, so it partly rewards the signal for agreeing with itself (mild circularity / look-back coupling). At weight 0.25 this is the most questionable component. Not look-*ahead* biased (it uses completed bars), but its *meaning* is ambiguous.
- **Momentum persistence (weight 0.20) — legitimate but redundant.** Fraction of the last N bars whose direction matches the window's net change. Reasonable, but it is highly correlated with ER (both measure "directional consistency"), so it adds less independent information than its weight implies.

Net: TQI is **~55% legitimate signal (ER + vol regime) and ~45% weak/redundant surface (structure + momentum-persist).** As a *regime measure* the ER and vol-regime parts are worth adopting; the structure factor I would drop or down-weight.

**SuperTrend-flip entry: laggy and whipsaw-prone on EURUSD intraday.** The entry is a SuperTrend direction flip confirmed on bar close. This is the same trend-following weakness covered in §2: *"the flip requires a close beyond the band — by which point price has already moved a good chunk of ATR"* (lag), and *"Supertrend can whipsaw you to death in sideways ranges"* (chop) ([TrendSpider](https://trendspider.com/learning-center/supertrend-indicator-a-comprehensive-guide/)). On a low-efficiency major like EURUSD outside the overlap, an unfiltered flip entry is a cost-bleed machine. The TQI band-modulation softens this but does not fix the fundamental issue that the *entry trigger* fires late and often.

**Character-flip detection: clever but unvalidated curve-fit surface.** Letting the SuperTrend flip when TQI collapses (prevTQI>0.55 → TQI<0.25 after a min trend age) rather than only on a price break is a reasonable *intent* — exit failing trends earlier. But it introduces four tunable parameters (min age, high threshold, low threshold, and the implicit interaction with band width) with no validation, and "flip on quality collapse" can itself fire on transient noise, *creating* whipsaws it was meant to avoid. This is added complexity with unproven edge — classic curve-fit risk. Do not adopt without a dedicated backtest proving it beats a plain price-break exit.

**Self-learning auto-calibration: correctly OFF, leave it off.** The 3×3 ER×vol grid storing EWMA realized-R per cell, nudging "quality influence," is explicitly OFF by default and labeled unvalidated. It is an in-sample adaptive loop with no out-of-sample guarantee — exactly the kind of thing that overfits. Ignore for the first build; if any adaptive calibration is ever wanted it belongs in the R5 improvement loop under strict walk-forward validation, not baked into the live signal.

**Repainting / look-ahead:** the script gates signals on `barstate.isconfirmed`, which is the correct non-repaint discipline (*"using barstate.isconfirmed is one way to avoid repainting"* — [TradingView repainting docs](https://www.tradingview.com/pine-script-docs/concepts/repainting/)). The SuperTrend bands themselves use prior-bar references, so the core is non-repainting *on confirmed bars*. The display score and dashboard are cosmetic. No obvious look-ahead in the entry logic — good. **But:** the on-chart "Performance Stats" table is computed from idealized fills with **no spread, no commission, no slippage, and no real position accounting** — it is not a backtest and must not be cited as evidence of edge. This is the single biggest trap: the script *looks* rigorous (self-aware, adaptive, self-learning) but contains zero cost modeling.

**Overall verdict.** **Mine, don't port.** Adopt the *idea* that drives TQI — a continuous ER + volatility-regime quality score — as the basis of our deterministic regime gate (§3), and discard the rest: do not build the SuperTrend-flip entry as our first candidate, do not adopt character-flip or auto-calibration, and never treat its on-chart stats as validation. It is worth keeping as a **regime-filter inspiration and a possible second-candidate trend follower** (with the entry replaced by something less laggy and the whole thing cost-backtested), not as a strategy to implement as-is.

## 5. Timeframe vs costs: the math

All-in EURUSD round-trip cost on FTMO ≈ **~0.4 pip raw spread + ~$7/lot/side commission**. At $10/pip per standard lot, $7 commission ≈ 0.7 pip; round trip commission ≈ 1.4 pip-equivalent **per side counted once each way is already in the $7×2**, so the practical all-in is roughly **spread (0.4) + commission (~1.1 pip-equivalent round trip) ≈ ~1.1–1.5 pip ≈ ~$11–15 per round-trip lot.** Call it **~1.1 pip all-in** as the working figure the project uses. The question is: what timeframe makes the typical move-per-signal large enough that ~1.1 pip is a small tax rather than the whole trade.

Rough per-bar range (EURUSD ADR ≈ 62 pips):

- **1m:** typical bar range on the order of ~1–3 pips; a "move per signal" you could realistically capture might be ~3–6 pips. Against ~1.1 pip cost, the cost is **~20–35% of the gross move** before you even account for being wrong half the time. This only survives if a backtest proves a *strong, persistent* sub-minute edge — which the literature says is unlikely after costs (*"difficult to profitably exploit these weak patterns in the face of trading costs"* — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.16772v1)). **Avoid 1m/3m unless a backtest explicitly clears costs.**
- **5m:** typical move per breakout/trend signal in the overlap on the order of ~8–20 pips. Cost ~1.1 pip is **~6–14% of gross** — workable, but tight; demands a genuinely good entry and tight slippage control.
- **15m:** typical capturable move per signal ~15–40 pips (a single overlap impulse). Cost ~1.1 pip is **~3–7% of gross** — comfortable. The signal has room to be wrong sometimes and still net positive expectancy. 15m also produces **far fewer bars/signals**, which directly helps the ≤2,000-request/day budget (§6).

**Recommendation: start on 15m EURUSD.** It is the timeframe where ~1.1 pip cost is a single-digit-percent tax, where false-breakout filtering has enough bar context to work (a 15m ORB is widely cited as the practical sweet spot — *"a 15-minute ORB offers a balance between quick execution and trend confirmation"* — [LiteFinance](https://www.litefinance.org/blog/for-beginners/trading-strategies/opening-range-breakout-strategy/)), and where request budget is comfortable. 5m can be a later, cost-validated refinement; 1m/3m only if R6 proves the edge survives, which I would bet against.

## 6. Order-management & the ≤2,000-request/day budget

The FTMO ≤2,000 server-requests/day cap means **per-tick trailing is out of the question** — a per-tick trail on even one instrument can issue thousands of modify requests in a single active hour. The design rule is **bar-close cadence, step-trailing, not continuous trailing:**

- **Place the bracket once, on entry.** When the breakout confirms on 15m bar close, submit the entry plus its SL and TP(s) as a single bracket/OCO order (R3 platform permitting). That is ~1–2 requests, not a stream.
- **Update the stop only on bar close, and only when it actually moves.** Recompute the trailing stop at each completed 15m bar; issue a modify request *only if the new stop differs meaningfully from the current one* (e.g. moved ≥ some ATR fraction). On 15m there are at most 96 bars per 24h, and the bot is flat most of them, so realistic modify volume is a few dozen requests/day — orders of magnitude under budget.
- **Step-trail, not pixel-trail.** Trail in discrete steps (e.g. move SL to breakeven after TP1, then ratchet by ½-ATR on each subsequent bar that makes a new favorable close). Discrete steps both respect the request budget and reduce noise-driven premature stop-outs.
- **Exit on bar close, not intrabar.** Let TP brackets fill server-side (no requests), and handle discretionary/regime-collapse exits on bar close. This keeps the request count deterministic and predictable.

With 15m bars, one instrument, bar-close-only stop updates, and most of the day spent flat, the request budget is a non-issue — which is itself an argument for 15m over 1m, where a busy day could approach the cap from order management alone.

## 7. Shortlist of candidate strategies

All three below share **two mandatory conditioning layers**: a **session gate** (only trade the London open / London-NY overlap window, flat in Asia and at rollover) and a **regime gate** (ER + ATR-ratio, ADX confirm). The candidates differ in the core trigger.

**Candidate A (recommended first build) — Session-gated overlap volatility breakout.**
*Trigger:* define an opening range (e.g. the first 2–4 bars of the London/overlap window, or a recent N-bar high/low channel); enter on a confirmed 15m close beyond the range **only if** ER > ER_min and ATR-ratio shows expansion. *Stop:* pivot ± SlMult·ATR (the reference script's SL construction is actually reasonable here). *Exit:* fixed R-multiple TP(s) (e.g. TP1 at ~1R for partial, runner trailed by step-trail to ~2–3R). *Market conditions:* needs the overlap volatility expansion; stands flat otherwise. *Rough expectancy hypothesis:* win rate ~45–55%, average ~1.2–1.8R on winners, target **expectancy ~+0.2 to +0.4R after costs** ([adventuresofgreg](https://adventuresofgreg.com/blog/2026/01/19/ultimate-guide-profitability-win-loss-ratios/); ORB win rates 40–60% per [LiteFinance](https://www.litefinance.org/blog/for-beginners/trading-strategies/opening-range-breakout-strategy/)). *Rationale:* it is built on the one genuinely-documented, time-localized FX edge (session volatility expansion), it is the approach whose main weakness (false breakouts) is exactly what our regime gate mitigates, and it is the most forgiving to debug.

**Candidate B (second) — Regime-filtered trend-follow (SuperTrend/Donchian-trail), gate-first.**
This is the reference-script *family*, rebuilt correctly: keep a TQI-style ER+vol regime gate as a *hard precondition*, replace the laggy SuperTrend-flip entry with either a pullback-in-trend entry or a tightened breakout, and cost-backtest everything. *Market conditions:* sustained overlap trends. *Rough expectancy hypothesis:* classic trend profile — **win rate ~35–45%, reward:risk ~2.5:1–4:1, expectancy ~+0.2 to +0.5R** if and only if the regime gate keeps it out of chop ([adventuresofgreg](https://adventuresofgreg.com/blog/2026/01/19/ultimate-guide-profitability-win-loss-ratios/)). *Rationale:* complementary to A (A captures the initial impulse, B rides the continuation); but it is more whipsaw-exposed and should follow A.

**Candidate C (later, lower priority) — Mean-reversion fade, tightly regime-gated.**
Fade overextensions toward a band/VWAP **only** in confirmed low-ER, range-bound conditions, with a hard stop and strict time/loss caps. *Market conditions:* the choppy majority of the session — but only when the regime gate confirms range, never in expansion. *Expectancy hypothesis:* high win rate (60–75%) but small average win and a dangerous left tail ([Axiory](https://www.axiory.com/trading-resources/strategies/mean-reversion-vs-trend-following), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.16772v1)). *Rationale:* deferred because its fat left tail is the worst fit for the 5%-daily / 10%-max envelope; only worth building once A/B are stable and the regime gate is proven to reliably exclude trend days.

### Recommendation: build Candidate A first

**Build the session-gated London/NY-overlap 15m EURUSD volatility breakout, hard-gated by the ER + ATR-ratio (+ADX confirm) regime filter, with pivot±ATR stops, an R-multiple TP1 partial, and a bar-close step-trailed runner.** It is the candidate whose edge rests on a *real, documented, time-localized phenomenon* rather than folklore; its known failure mode (false breakouts) is precisely what the regime gate exists to suppress; it sits comfortably on 15m where ~1.1-pip costs are a single-digit tax and the request budget is trivial; and it directly embodies the project's hard-won lesson — **regime-aware first, signal second.** Mine the reference script's TQI (ER + vol regime) for the gate, discard its SuperTrend-flip entry and character-flip/self-learning machinery, and never trust its on-chart stats.

### Fit inside the FTMO envelope (sanity check)

Per R4 the Risk Governor sizes ~0.35% equity (~$350 on $100k) per trade. At +0.3R expectancy with ~0.35% risk/trade, and standing flat most of the day, a realistic intraday cadence of a handful of trades/day keeps **daily P&L variance well inside the 5% daily / 10% max limits** even on a losing cluster: e.g. five consecutive full-stop losses ≈ 5 × 0.35% ≈ 1.75% — comfortably under the 5% daily wall, with the Risk Governor's daily-loss kill-switch as the hard backstop. Worst-case drawdown should be stress-tested in R6 against losing streaks of 8–12 (plausible at a 45–55% win rate), and the rule of thumb is to plan for **~1.5× the backtested max drawdown** in live conditions ([adventuresofgreg](https://adventuresofgreg.com/blog/2026/01/19/ultimate-guide-profitability-win-loss-ratios/)). These are hypotheses for R6 to confirm or kill — not promises.
